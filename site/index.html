Architectural principles

Honest telemetry instead of fake green checkmarks

Most kill switches equate a saved toggle with active protection. VPN Sentinel separates user intent from verified enforcement at every layer.

  • Intent vs. confirmation

    Selecting a rule expresses protection intent, not proof of filtering. Whenever enforcement confirmation is absent, the state is explicitly shown as Unconfirmed.

  • Companion for existing clients

    Works alongside PureVPN, WireGuard, OpenVPN, remote SOCKS5/HTTP CONNECT proxies and local Mac proxy apps instead of replacing your tunnel client.

  • Device denials win first

    Individual device blocks (such as a NAS or printer) take strict priority over both local network trust and the 15-minute protection pause.

  • Bounded 15-minute pause

    Temporary direct internet access never stays forgotten forever. The pause expires in 15 minutes and ends immediately on network change, reboot or manual resume.

  • Episode-based outage alerts

    Requests a single alert sound when a permitted tunnel drops and a silent notification after 5 stable seconds of recovery—preventing alert storms.

  • Deterministic diagnostics

    Freezes an immutable in-memory snapshot so you can review every record and toggle optional details before exporting a demo-marked JSON file.

Policy & controls matrix

Decision precedence and operator controls

Every packet and state transition in SentinelPolicy follows a deterministic, auditable priority order.

Policy evaluation precedence (1 → 5)

1. Device denial
Explicitly denied LAN devices (NAS, printer) are blocked even in a trusted network or during a pause
2. Trusted LAN
Permits direct access only to non-denied local devices on an explicitly trusted network
3. 15-min pause
Allows temporary direct internet access until timer expiry, network switch, reboot or resume
4. Confirmed tunnel
Routes traffic through the explicitly selected VPN, remote proxy or local Mac proxy with active confirmation
5. Fail-closed gate
Denies direct internet bypass whenever the selected tunnel is unavailable or unconfirmed

Questions & boundaries

Frequently asked questions

Does the current version block real internet traffic or prevent leaks yet?

No. Version 0.1.0 (build 1) is an interactive demonstration workbench and architecture validation milestone. Both the Demo app and the pass-through Experimental bundle explicitly state “Traffic blocking is not active” and never modify your Mac’s routes, VPN, DNS or firewall.

What is the difference between VPN-Sentinel-Demo.zip and VPN-Sentinel-Experiment.zip?

VPN-Sentinel-Demo.zip contains only the standalone SwiftUI menu bar workbench (com.vpnsentinel.demo) without any system extension or entitlements. VPN-Sentinel-Experiment.zip (com.vpnsentinel.experiment) additionally embeds the signed and notarized pass-through NetworkExtension packet filter used for signing and packaging verification.

Why does VPN Sentinel distinguish between “protection intent” and “blocking confirmation”?

A saved setting or a green icon in a VPN client does not prove that packets cannot leave your Mac directly. In VPN Sentinel, protection is only shown as active when both a permitted connection is selected and current enforcement confirmation is present; otherwise the state is shown as Unconfirmed.

How do Trusted LAN and individual device denials interact?

Each network starts untrusted. You can explicitly trust a network for local access, while still denying specific devices such as a NAS or printer. In SentinelPolicy, an explicit device denial has higher priority than both network trust and the 15-minute pause.

How does the 15-minute protection pause work?

When you start a pause, direct internet access is permitted in the model for up to 15 minutes. Switching to another network, rebooting, or clicking “End pause” terminates the pause immediately so direct access is never left open by accident.

Does the app collect telemetry, analytics or network logs in the background?

No. The app makes zero network requests, sends no telemetry, and stores its demo session only in memory. Diagnostic export writes a JSON file only when you explicitly click Export and choose a destination in the macOS Save panel.

How can I verify the Apple notarization and signature on my Mac?

Run shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS, then check the extracted app with codesign --verify --deep --strict --all-architectures, xcrun stapler validate, and spctl --assess --type execute --verbose=4.
