Your VPN dropped.Your internet didn’t.

When a VPN or proxy drops, macOS quietly sends traffic straight out. VPN Sentinel is a sentinel in your menu bar, built to keep that direct exit closed — and to tell you once that the channel is gone.

01 — The problem

A VPN drop is invisible. So are its consequences.

Your browser, messengers and background services don’t know the tunnel is gone. They just keep working — through your ISP.

  1. 01

    The kill switch lives inside the VPN client

    If the client hangs, updates, or you switch providers, the protection goes with it. Sentinel runs separately, next to any client.

  2. 02

    “Connected” proves nothing

    The client’s icon, a utun interface, a successful ping — these are signals, not a guarantee that every packet goes through the tunnel.

  3. 03

    A leak is more than your IP

    DNS queries, IPv6, UDP and macOS background services. Anything that bypassed the chosen channel is a leak — even if it’s encrypted.

02 — The idea

Pressing “protect” doesn’t make you protected.

Intent

Protection on

You said this. The setting is saved — but it proves nothing yet.

≠

Confirmation

  • the rules are actually applied by the system
  • for the current network and connection
  • checked now, not at launch

The system verified this.

Sentinel says “protected” only when both are true. Without confirmation it says so: “Blocking not confirmed”. No green checkmarks “just in case” — which is why there’s no green in the app or on this site.

03 — Menu bar

Four states. None of them lie.

Sentinel’s icon is a barrier. It shows what’s happening to the direct exit without opening any window.

Channel working

Barrier down, blue light. Internet only through the VPN or proxy you chose.

Via “My VPN” · blocking confirmed

Channel lost

Barrier down, light off. The direct exit stays closed until reconnect. One chime per outage.

VPN unavailable · direct internet closed

Not confirmed

Dashed barrier, red light. Sentinel can’t verify the blocking is in effect — and doesn’t pretend it can.

Blocking not confirmed · check

Pause

Barrier up, amber light. You opened the direct exit yourself — a timer runs next to it.

Direct access · 12:41 left

never

Sentinel won’t say “Traffic blocked” without confirmation, or show “Protected” just because your VPN client says “Connected”.

Set it up once. Then it stays quiet — until something happens you actually need to know about.

    Pick your VPN — once

    In Sentinel’s menu you mark which VPN or proxy may reach the internet. A new connection never gets trust on its own. You mark your home network as trusted — the printer and NAS stay reachable.

    via “My VPN”
  2. 11:47

    The VPN drops mid-call

    The icon changes, one chime sounds, a notification arrives. Direct internet is closed, but the printer still prints: the trusted network stays reachable.

  3. 11:48

    The client reconnects by itself

    After 5 stable seconds Sentinel quietly reports the channel is back. No sound — good news shouldn’t interrupt.

    restored · silent
  4. 14:30

    Your bank’s site blocks VPNs

    You start a 15-minute pause. The timer sits in the menu bar; one button brings protection back early.

    pause · 15:00
  5. 14:45

    The pause ends by itself

    Protection returns without you. A pause also ends on network change or restart — you can’t forget it on.

    via “My VPN”
  6. 16:10

    The filter didn’t answer after an update

    Sentinel doesn’t know whether the rules are in effect — and shows it with a red dashed barrier, not a green checkmark. It tries to restore blocking; until confirmed, you see the problem.

  7. 18:20

    The network isn’t trusted: its devices are closed until you decide otherwise. Internet still goes only through your VPN.

05 — Rules

Who decides when rules conflict

Top to bottom: the first matching rule wins. Anything not explicitly allowed is closed.

  1. Device denialA denied NAS stays unreachable even on a trusted network and during a pause.closed
  2. Trusted local networkThe printer and other devices on a network you marked yourself.local
  3. PauseDirect internet, if you opened it. No longer than 15 minutes.direct
  4. Confirmed channelThe VPN or proxy you chose, with its operation confirmed.via VPN
  5. Everything elseChannel lost, unconfirmed or unknown — the direct exit is closed.closed

09 — Questions

The short version

Does the current version protect against leaks?

No. Version 0.1 demonstrates the logic and verifies the build. Neither the demo nor the experimental build changes routes, DNS, VPN or filtering rules on your Mac. Blocking arrives after the mechanism passes external measurements.

How is this different from my VPN’s kill switch?

A built-in kill switch depends on a specific client and goes away with it. Sentinel is designed as a separate utility: you choose the permitted connection, and it makes sure nothing bypasses it — even if you switch clients.

Will it work with my VPN?

PureVPN and one more independent client are tested first, plus remote and local proxies (SOCKS5, HTTP CONNECT). “Works with any client” is not promised: every configuration has to be verified.

What about my printer, NAS and AirPlay?

On a network you explicitly marked as trusted, local devices are reachable. Individual devices can be denied. On a new or public network, local access is closed until you decide otherwise.

Why is the pause only 15 minutes?

So the direct exit can’t be opened “for a minute” and forgotten. The pause ends on its own, and also on network change or restart. One button brings protection back early.

Why is there no green on this site?

Because the core idea of the project is that a green checkmark proves nothing. Colours in Sentinel only mean the state of the traffic path: blue is the tunnel, red is a direct exit or a problem, amber is a pause.
